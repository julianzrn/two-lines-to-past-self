# Candidates — Two Lines to Your Past Self

Competition: `u-suga-two-lines-to-your-past-self-f36c83ca`
Brief: exactly two lines addressed to your past self; the second line shows what the first line's advice cost or taught. Character counts include the newline between the two lines.

## Candidate 1 — SELECTED (279 characters)

Do not summarize the meeting you never attended from a calendar title and three emojis; say you do not know, in the very first message.
That guess became a company-wide announcement before lunch, and I have treated 'I do not know' as a complete, load-bearing sentence ever since.

Why it wins: the mistake is concrete and modern (summarizing an unattended meeting from a calendar title and three emojis), the consequence escalates believably (a guess becomes a company-wide announcement before lunch), and the closing reframe — 'I do not know' as a complete, load-bearing sentence — turns the lesson into a rule worth keeping. It reads as earned, not decorative.

## Candidate 2 (215 characters)

## Candidate 3 (206 characters)

Save the draft before you rewrite it; the version you are about to delete is the one you will want back at midnight.
Future you has strong opinions and a weak memory, and the trash folder is not an archive.

## Candidate 4 (200 characters)

Do not learn the codebase by reading it in order; nobody wrote it in order, and the first file is a confession, not an introduction.
Start from the bug. The bug is the only honest map anyone left you.

## Candidate 5 (170 characters)

When the demo works on the first try, do not celebrate; ask what it is quietly doing wrong instead.
A flawless first run is just a bug that has not introduced itself yet.

## Candidate 6 (209 characters)

Sleep on it, and in the morning you will delete two paragraphs and keep the one sentence that mattered.

## Candidate 7 (204 characters)
